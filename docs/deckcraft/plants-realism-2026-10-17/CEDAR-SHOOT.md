## What the photos are for

| What you shoot | What DeckCraft gets | In plain words |
|---|---|---|
| Sprays lit from below on the light pad | **Alpha** and **translucency** | Alpha is the exact cut-out outline of each spray. Translucency is how much light glows through when the sun is behind the hedge. |
| Sprays lit from above through crossed polarisers | **Albedo** | The true colour of the foliage, with all shine removed. |
| The same, with the polarisers lined up (parallel) | **Gloss / roughness** | How shiny the scales are. |
| One lamp moved low around the pad to 8 positions | **Normals** | The tiny bumps and ridges, so light catches them correctly. |
| The colour chart, in the top-lit pad setups and at every outdoor setup | **Colour calibration** | It cancels out the colour quirks of the phone, the lamps and the day, so the green is your real green. The backlit shots use the empty pad as their reference instead, because a chart can't be lit from behind. |
| Whole hedge from fixed spots, with a height marker | **Shape, size and the photo-match test** | I render DeckCraft from the same spots and put the two side by side. That comparison is the pass/fail test for the cedar pilot. |
| The hedge with the sun behind it | **A real-world check of the glow colour** | The light pad's own colour is uncertain, so this checks that it is right. |
| Measurements | **True size** | Height, how the width tapers, and spray size, so the 3D cedar has the right proportions. |

## 2. Phone settings

### Which lens: always the 1x main camera
- The 1x is the sharpest lens and has the biggest sensor. On iPhone it's the only lens that takes 48-megapixel RAW.
- DeckCraft's 3D camera sees a narrower view than the 1x (38° top to bottom). So I can crop your 1x photo to match it exactly, but I can't widen a zoomed one.
- **Don't** pinch-zoom, use 0.5x (it distorts), or use 2x and above.
- **iPhone 15 Pro and later:** tapping the 1x button cycles through 28 mm and 35 mm crops. These change the view and aren't full 48 MP. Set the default in the table below, and don't tap the 1x button again once you're on it. **[verify on your model]**
- Hold the phone **sideways (landscape)** for the hedge shots. DeckCraft's screen is landscape.

### iPhone Pro models (12 Pro and later): ProRAW
"RAW" saves what the sensor saw instead of a finished JPEG, so colour and exposure can be corrected exactly later. Apple's version is called ProRAW.

The menu names below come from Apple's iOS 17-era help. **[unsure]** They may be worded slightly differently on your iOS version.

| Setting | Where | Set to |
|---|---|---|
| ProRAW | Settings > Camera > Formats > **ProRAW & Resolution Control** | **On**, and choose **48 MP** (ProRAW Max) on the 14 Pro and later. In the Camera app, tap the **RAW** badge at the top so it reads **RAW MAX**. |
| Main lens (15 Pro and later) | Settings > Camera > **Main Camera** | Default lens **24 mm**. Turn off the extra 28 mm and 35 mm lenses if the option is there. **[unsure of exact wording]** |
| Keep your choices | Settings > Camera > **Preserve Settings** | Turn on **ProRAW & Resolution Control**, **Exposure Adjustment**, **Macro Control**, and **Night Mode** if it's listed **[unsure]**. |
| Grid and level | Settings > Camera > **Grid** and **Level** | Both **on**. When the phone points straight down, two crosshairs appear and turn yellow when it's flat. When you shoot forward, Level shows only side-to-side tilt, not up/down tilt. Use the torpedo level for that (Part 1). |
| Macro auto-switch | Settings > Camera > **Macro Control** | **On**, so the flower icon appears in the Camera app. Then **tap the flower to turn macro off** whenever it shows. Otherwise, close up, the phone silently switches to the weaker 0.5x lens. |
| Photographic Style | Settings > Camera > Photographic Styles | **Standard** (no style) |
| Burst on volume button | Settings > Camera > **Use Volume Up for Burst** | **Off**. The Bluetooth remote acts as the volume button, and burst mode can't save RAW. |
| Faster shooting | Settings > Camera > **Prioritize Faster Shooting** | **Off**. It lowers quality when you shoot quickly. |
| In the Camera app | top bar | Flash **off**. Night mode **off** (the moon icon); check it before every set, because it switches itself on in a dark room. Live Photo turns off by itself in RAW. No Portrait mode. |
| Screen lock, pad session only | Settings > Display & Brightness > **Auto-Lock** | **Never** during the light-pad session, then set it back. If the phone locks, the exposure lock is lost, and unlocking means touching the phone. **[unsure]** The Camera app may keep the screen awake anyway. |
| Location on client sites | Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services > Camera | **Never** while at a client's home, to keep their address out of the files. |
| Transfer format | Settings > Apps > Photos (older iOS: Settings > Photos) > Transfer to Mac or PC | **Keep Originals** |

**HDR and "beauty" processing:**
- These change colour and contrast differently in every frame, so frames can't be compared.
- Older iPhones (XS–12) have a **Smart HDR** switch. Turn it **off** if you have it.
- **[unsure]** On the iPhone 13 and later it can't be switched off, and Apple also merges several frames inside ProRAW. ProRAW stays close enough to raw to be colour-corrected with the chart. That's why the chart is used throughout and why exposure stays locked.

**Locking exposure and focus.** In RAW, white balance (the colour of the light) doesn't need locking, because it's fixed later from the chart.
1. **Press and hold** on the subject until the yellow **AE/AF LOCK** banner appears. That locks both exposure and focus.
2. To change brightness, tap the **^** arrow at the top, then the **±** button, and slide to the value you want (for example −1.0, 0.0 or +2.0). The bracket steps in the shot list use this.
   - "EV" means one step of brightness: −1 is half as bright, +1 is twice as bright.
   - Because Preserve Settings keeps the ± value, **set ± back to 0 after every set**. Otherwise a leftover +2 carries into the next shot, and even into the next day.
3. Fire with the **Bluetooth remote**, not the screen.
4. Wait about 2 seconds between shots, because 48 MP RAW takes a moment to save.
5. **[unsure]** Opening the photo you just took may drop the lock. Check your pictures at the end of each set, not in the middle.
6. **ISO** is the phone's brightness boost: higher means grainier. To see it, open a photo in Photos and swipe up (or tap ⓘ). The dry run uses this.

**Space:** Apple quotes about **75 MB per 48 MP ProRAW photo**, and about 25 MB at 12 MP. This shoot is about 145 photos required, up to about 180 with the extras, plus the dry run. **Free up about 15 GB on the phone (20 GB to be safe).** If iCloud Photos is on, these photos will also count against your iCloud storage.

### Phones without RAW: a fallback only
- **The light-pad station needs RAW.** Without RAW:
  - the stock iPhone app can't lock white balance (the AE/AF lock doesn't hold it);
  - frames full of foliage pull the automatic white balance off;
  - HEIF and JPEG are processed differently in every frame, so even with the chart, the colour and bump-map inputs can't be made reliable.
- **Options, best first:**
  1. Borrow a ProRAW iPhone or a Samsung with Expert RAW for the shoot.
  2. On Android, use Pro mode with RAW on and white balance fixed to a number (for example 5500 K) or to "Daylight".
  3. Use a free manual-white-balance DNG camera app, such as Adobe Lightroom's built-in camera. **[unsure]** I couldn't confirm it's free today, and it needs an Adobe sign-in, which is your decision.
- If you do shoot outdoors without RAW, **take a chart frame at every camera position**, not just at the start and end.

### Android
- **Samsung Galaxy S, Note or Fold:**
  - Use **Expert RAW**, a free Samsung app from the Galaxy Store. Installing it is your call. It saves DNG files (a RAW format) and lets you set brightness, white balance and focus by hand.
  - Or use **Pro mode** with RAW copies turned on (Camera settings > Advanced picture options > Picture formats > RAW copies).
  - Turn **Scene optimizer** and any AI or auto-HDR enhancement off.
- **Google Pixel:**
  - Camera > Settings > Advanced > **RAW / JPEG control** on, then choose **RAW+JPEG**.
  - The Pixel 8 Pro and later also have **Pro controls** for manual exposure, white balance and focus.
- **All Android phones:**
  - Turn on Grid and Level, and turn off beauty/face retouch and flash.
  - Make the volume key take a photo, so the remote works.
  - Use the 1x lens.
  - Set the screen timeout to its longest for the pad session.
- **[unsure]** Menu names vary by model and Android version.
- Some Samsung DNGs have shown a colour tint in other software. The chart handles that, but tell me the exact phone model.

## 3. Where to get the plants

### The reference hedge (for the whole-plant photos)
Pick **one established 'Smaragd' hedge** that you can come back to for the sunny visit. Ideally:
- **Confirmed 'Smaragd'**, from your invoice or the tag. If you're not sure, write that down.
- **About 2.4–3 m (8–10 ft) tall.** DeckCraft draws them at 8 ft and the estate rows at about 9.3 ft, about 2–3 ft wide.
- **Healthy and typically kept**, in a **mulched bed**, so the base and mulch shots are useful.
- **The face you'll photograph points roughly southeast to southwest**, so it's in sun around midday for Part 3. A north-facing face gets no direct sun in November.
- **Clear lawn:**
  - 5 m or more straight out from the hedge centre;
  - 7–8 m out at a 45° angle from the centre;
  - room to stand a few metres past one end, in line with the hedge, for the end-on profile photo.
- **A raised viewpoint 10–25 m away and at least 2.5 m up**, about 35° off square: a deck, a porch or an upstairs window. This is for the "DeckCraft Corner" shot.
- **Both faces reachable**, for sunny and shaded sprays and for the sun-behind-the-hedge shots.
- Few people, cars or house numbers in the background. The photos stay private anyway.

### Sources, best first
1. **A client hedge you installed, with permission.** It's the best whole-plant reference, and you know its history. Get an OK in writing (a text is fine) that matches what you'll actually do. Here's a draft you can send or edit:
   > Hi ___, could I stop by on ___ for about an hour and a half to photograph your cedar hedge for our 3D design software, then come back for 30 minutes on a sunny day the following week? I'd also like a few photos from your [deck / porch / upstairs window], if that's OK. I'd snip about 14 small green tips (hand-sized), one per plant, spread along both sides at waist-to-shoulder height so it won't show, plus a little brown foliage and a short twig from inside. I'd never cut into the brown wood, so the hedge fills back in. The photos stay private: no house, people or address shown. Thanks!

   If the client only allows cutting from the back, take the sprays from your own stock or a supplier, and use the client hedge only for photos.
2. **Your own stock or yard.** Fine for sprays, but nursery-sized plants are juvenile, so they can't serve as the whole-hedge reference.
3. **A supplier yard.** Ask before cutting. If they'd rather you buy a plant, that's a purchase and your decision.

Using the **same hedge for the sprays and the whole-plant photos** is best, because then the leaf colour and the hedge match.

### Cutting the sprays
- **Where:** from the hedge face at about **1.0–1.6 m up**, the height people see.
  - **Take one spray per plant**, spread along the hedge.
  - Favour the end plants and less-seen spots on each face.
- **How many:** cut **3–5 from the sunny face and 3–5 from the shaded face, plus 2 spares of each.** Sun-grown and shade-grown foliage differ in colour, and the 3D cedar uses both.
- **Cut only green tips, never into brown wood.** Cedar doesn't regrow from bare brown wood, so a deep cut leaves a hole that lasts.
- **What a good spray looks like:**
  - a typical flat fan spray, about **10–20 cm**, cut with a little stem;
  - outer, healthy green foliage;
  - no winter burn, insect damage or broken tips.
- **Optional extras:** 1–2 pieces of the **brown inside foliage** and a 5 cm **twig** with bark, taken from inside where it won't show.
  - Inner foliage turning golden-brown and dropping in Sept–Oct is normal for cedars (Iowa State and Wisconsin Extension).
  - That browning is real, so photograph it on the plant, but cut your main sprays from the green outside.
- **Cut last, just before you leave the hedge.**
  - Use **separate bags labelled SUN and SHADE**, each with a damp (not wet) paper towel.
  - Keep them in a shaded cooler, with no ice touching them.
- **Shoot them within about 2 hours of cutting**, so the light-pad room should be **within about 30 minutes' drive** of the hedge.
  - Scanning studios note that cut plants change quickly.
  - **[unsure]** Cedar probably holds up better than soft leaves, but I don't know how fast its colour shifts.
  - If the pad session slips to the evening, keep the sprays cool and note the delay.
- **Pat them dry** before shooting. Water droplets make false shine.

### Part 1: at the hedge (overcast)

**For all of Part 1 (and Part 3): take the polariser clip OFF the phone.** It changes the sheen on the foliage, and the 3D render has no polariser, so the comparison would be off. Location stays OFF on client sites.

**Step 1. Measure and mark: (i) measurements, about 25–30 min.** Write everything on the sheet at the end of this document, and photograph the tape for about 6 of the readings.
- **Pick 3 typical plants** in the hedge. For each one, tape:
  - **height**, from the mulch to the top tip;
  - **depth front-to-back** at the **base** (30 cm up) and the **middle** (half height).
  - Measure **width along the row** only on an **end plant** or a free-standing specimen. Inside a closed hedge the plants interlock, so it can't be measured.
  - Don't climb to measure the top. The end-on photo and the Front photo give depth and taper at every height.
- **End-on profile photo, 2 frames:**
  - Stand in line with the hedge, a few metres past one end.
  - Put the rod upright at the end plant.
  - Phone on the tripod at 1.6 m, level, and the whole hedge profile in frame.
- **For the whole hedge, record:**
  - total length, number of plants, and spacing centre to centre;
  - the height where the foliage starts above the mulch (the "skirt");
  - mulch type, colour and depth, the edging type, and the distance from the hedge base to the bed edge.
- **Measure 10 sprays still on the plant:** the length from the fan tip to where it joins the branch.
- **Background facts:**
  - **Which way the face points**, from the phone's Compass app, standing facing the hedge, a few metres from cars and metal. It's only a rough guide (it can be off by 5–10°).
  - Roughly how many hours of sun each face gets.
  - Age or planting year, planted size, and when it was last sheared, if known.
- **Mark the hedge and the camera spots:**
  - Put a flag at each end plant: **A** (left) and **B** (right). On a long hedge, use two flagged plants about 3 m apart instead.
  - Put a landscape flag at each camera spot.
  - For each spot, tape the **distance from A to the spot and from B to the spot** (to the tripod centre), plus the **lens height above the ground there**. Two distances pin the spot exactly, so the sunny visit and the 3D camera use the same place.
  - Don't rely on the compass for camera spots.
  - If you can't leave flags, also measure from fixed things like a fence post or the house corner.

**Step 2. Whole hedge, overcast: (g), about 35 min.**
- **Set-up for every position:**
  - tripod; landscape orientation; 1x; clip off;
  - lock exposure and focus on the hedge.
- **Checking level:** for "level" shots, hold the torpedo level's **plumb (vertical) vial against the back of the phone**. When the bubble is centred, the phone isn't tilted up or down. The phone's Level line only checks side-to-side tilt.
- **Marker:** stand the rod, with its tape bands every 50 cm, **upright at the hedge base at the centre of the face**. It gives a known height, and the bands let me work out the ground slope and camera tilt later.
- **Each position, 4 frames:**
  - 2 with the marker;
  - then remove it and take 2 clean;
  - if it's breezy, take extras and wait for a lull.

| Position | Where | Lens height | Aim |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Corner** (required) | At **45°** to the hedge line, **6–8 m** from the hedge centre (7 m is ideal). Record the A and B distances. | **1.6 m** | Level. Hedge centre on the centre of the grid. |
| **Front** (required) | Square-on, **5 m** from the face. Step back if the hedge doesn't fit, and record the distances. | **1.6 m** | Level. |
| **Bed close-up** (required) | Square-on, **3 m** from the hedge base | **1.5 m** | Tilt down so the centre of the frame is **45 cm up the hedge**. Tie a bit of flagging tape there to aim at. This is the same height and aim as DeckCraft's Bed close-up camera (60 in high, 120 in back, aimed 18 in up). DeckCraft measures that from the bed centre, so the distance may differ slightly. |
| **DeckCraft Corner** (required) | About **35° off square**, **10–25 m** from the hedge centre, from the client's deck, porch or an upstairs window (with permission). **Not from the top of a ladder.** | About **2.5–3.5 m** | Looking down about 15°, with the hedge centre on the grid centre. Note where you stood (for example, "deck, NE corner by the post"), and tape or pace the distances to A and B if you can. The rod's bands let me work out the exact angle. |
| Corner, other end (optional) | Mirror image of the first Corner | 1.6 m | Level. |
| Above (optional, **only if safe**) | Step ladder **3–4 m** out, lens about 2.5–3 m up, looking down 30–45°. Firm, level ground; never the top two steps; someone holding the ladder. | Note it | Shows the hedge top, as seen from a deck or upstairs. |

- **Chart:** 1 frame at the start and 1 at the end.
  - Hold the chart **flat against (parallel to) the hedge face** at chest height. Tilt it slightly if it shows glare.
  - Shoot it square-on from about 1.5 m, so it fills about a third of the frame, **near the middle of the frame**.
- **How these compare with DeckCraft's own views:**
  - Corner, Front and Bed close-up here are **custom cameras**: I'll save a DeckCraft camera at exactly the spot where you stood.
  - DeckCraft's built-in Corner button sits about 35° off square. It is higher (about 2.5 m for a typical deck) and further back (8 m or more), looking about 15° down. The DeckCraft Corner shot covers that view, which is where the realism of the whole canopy gets judged.
  - DeckCraft's built-in Front is lower (about 0.6× deck height, roughly 0.5 m for a typical deck) and further back.
  - Its Above is a straight-down plan view, which a ladder can't copy.
  - **What matters is that you record where you stood.**

**Step 3. Close-ups of the living hedge: (f), about 10 min.** Clip off. Use 1x at about 25–35 cm, turn macro off if the flower appears, and tap to focus. 12 frames in total:
- **Sunny face:** 3 frames, filling the frame with foliage.
- **Shaded face:** 3 frames.
- **Top of the hedge:** 2 frames.
- **Inside:** gently part the foliage. 2 frames showing the inner browning and the branch structure.
- **Edge against the sky:** 2 frames from the side or slightly below, showing the fuzzy outline against the overcast sky. The outline is one of the biggest "is it real?" cues.
- **Chart:** 1 frame in the same light.

**Step 4. Base, bark and mulch: (h), about 10 min.** Clip off. 8 frames, plus 1 chart frame:
- **Trunk base:** part the foliage at ground level (a helper or a bungee helps). 3 frames from 40–60 cm, showing the stems meeting the soil and mulch.
- **Bark close-up:** 2 frames of a main stem from 20–30 cm.
- **Base line:** lens about 40 cm high, 1.5 m away, looking along the hedge. 2 frames showing where the foliage skirt meets the mulch and edging.
- **Mulch:** 1 frame straight down.
- **Chart:** 1 frame.

**Step 5. Cut and bag the sprays** (see section 3). Then leave right away.

### Part 2: the light-pad station (indoors)

**Room:**
- A garage or room you can make **dark**: door closed, windows covered.
- Overhead lights stay **off** for every frame.
- The **only** light is whichever one the step says.

**Rig, set up the day before (about 30 min, including the polariser check):**
1. **Pad on the floor** on a flat board, plugged in, at **full brightness**. Dimmed LEDs can flicker and leave stripes.
2. **On the pad's margin**, outside where the sprays will go:
   - **4 small black-tape crosses** near the corners, as alignment marks;
   - the **clear plastic ruler** along one edge;
   - the **two chrome balls at opposite corners**, each sat on a small ring of tape so it can't roll.
   - These stay put all session.
   - Put the **open chart** and the **index-card label** on the floor right beside the pad. In layout frames, the chart is just an ID and a drift check, so it doesn't matter that it's dark in the backlit shots.
3. **Phone overhead, pointing straight down.**
   - **Main option: the tripod's side arm**, with the sandbag on. Ideally the legs sit outside the raking circle. If they can't, place them between raking spots, never in line with one.
   - **Second choice:** one straight 2×4, at least 2.4 m long, across two sawhorses that each stand at least 1.2 m from the pad centre. Tape the phone on top with its camera end hanging over the board's side edge, so the clip hangs in free air.
   - Don't lay the phone across two boards with a gap: the clip won't fit, and the gap would have to be wider than the phone.
   - Level it with the Camera crosshairs, and use 1x with macro off.
4. **Raise or lower** the phone until the frame holds the whole pad plus the chart and label beside it. At 1x this is roughly 50–75 cm above the pad **[approximate]**.
5. **Polariser film:**
   - Before cutting, **draw arrows on the film sheet**, all pointing the same way.
   - Cut one piece for each top lamp, and tape it over the lamp's front with the **arrow up and the same face out** on both.
   - If you don't have the NL-36AI, one BP66 also does the raking. Tape that lamp's film along the arrow edge only, as a **hinge**, so it flips up for raking and drops back to exactly the same angle afterwards.
6. **Lamp positions.** Tape numbered marks on the floor.
   - **Top lights "L" and "R":**
     - beside the phone, heads at about phone height, **25–35 cm either side of the lens** (about 65–75° above the pad), pointing at the pad centre;
     - both on the same plain white diffuser, with no coloured filter;
     - if they have a colour-temperature dial, set both to the same value (for example 5600 K) and tape it;
     - both at full brightness.
   - **Raking spots 1–8:** a circle around the pad centre, one spot every 45° (like a compass: N, NE, E and so on).
     - Use a **1.5 m** circle if the room allows and the dry-run ISO stays under about 800. Otherwise use **1 m**.
     - Lamp head height is about 0.85 × the distance (about 40° above the pad): **85 cm at 1 m, 1.3 m at 1.5 m**.
     - Point it at the pad centre, and use the same height and distance every time.
     - **Raking lamp:** the NL-36AI with no film, propped on a stool, shelf or stacked boxes at the taped height. Or use a BP66 on its stand with its film flipped up.
7. **Cross the polarisers.** "Crossed" means the lamp's and the lens's polarisers are turned 90° apart, which blocks the shine.
   - With L and R on and the pad off, look at the chrome balls on the phone screen.
   - Turn the clip-on polariser until the balls' bright dots go **dark**, and mark the ring with tape: this is **"X"**.
   - A quarter turn from it, where the dots are brightest, is **"="** (parallel).
   - **If one dot stays bright:**
     - check that both films have the arrow up and the same face out;
     - then slowly rotate that lamp's film, or tilt the lamp head, until it darkens.
   - If no dot goes dark at any angle, the clip may be on backwards.
8. **Phone:** Auto-Lock set to Never, Night mode off, ± at 0.

**Dry run, about 30 min, right after set-up:**
- Do one layout (steps below) with any evergreen sprig from the yard.
- **Check ISO** in the photo info:
  - If the crossed or parallel frames are above about 800, move L and R closer, so the heads are about 70–80 cm from the pad centre. The empty frames even out the light.
  - If the raking frames are above about 800, use the 1 m circle.
- **Check that the balls' dots show at all 8 raking spots.**
- **Check for shadows:** look at one frame from each raking spot and from L and R for shadows from the tripod legs or the boards.
- Put the photos in the shared folder (section 6). I'll check focus, exposure (the bare pad must not be blown out to pure white in the −1 backlit frame), the polariser crossing and the framing before the real shoot.

**Session reference frames, at the start.** The balls, ruler and tape crosses stay on the pad.

| Frame | Lights | Lens | On the pad | Frames |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Backlit, empty | Pad only | X | Bare | 1, at −1 |
| Chart, crossed | L+R | X | Chart lying flat in the middle of the pad, where the sprays go | 3: lock on the chart, shoot 0, −1, +1, then ± back to 0 |
| Chart, parallel | L+R | = | Same | 1 |
| Board, crossed | L+R | X | Matte white board inside the tape crosses (or the bare pad, if you have no board) | 1 |
| Board, parallel | L+R | = | Same | 1 |
| Raking, empty, spots 1–8 | Raking lamp, no film | Leave as is | Board if you have it, otherwise bare | 8, locked once at spot 1 |
| **Total** | | | | **15** |

At the very **end** of the session, take 3 more frames: one empty backlit frame, and the board under L+R at X and at =. These "empty" frames record how uneven the pad and lamps are, so it can be subtracted out. They also show whether anything drifted during the session.

**Layouts:**

| Layout | Label card | What's on the pad |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | `SMARAGD L1 SUN TOP` | The sunny-side sprays, top side up |
| L2 | `SMARAGD L2 SUN FLIP` | The same sprays turned over (undersides are usually paler) |
| L3 | `SMARAGD L3 SHADE TOP` | The shaded-side sprays, top side up |
| L4 | `SMARAGD L4 SHADE FLIP` | The shaded-side sprays turned over |
| L5 (optional) | `SMARAGD L5 BROWN+TWIG` | Brown inner pieces and the twig |

**Placing the sprays:**
- 2–3 sprays per layout, in the **central part of the pad inside the tape crosses**.
  - They must **not overlap**. Leave gaps of 2–3 cm between them, and 3 cm from the edge of the lit area.
  - A spray longer than about 25 cm goes alone, along the long side.
- Point the stems toward the bottom of the frame.
- If they spring up, weigh down the **stem ends only** with coins.
- Use glass only if they still won't lie flat, and only real glass that passed the rainbow test. Write **GLASS** on the label card. That layout's parallel (c) and raking (d) frames will be unusable because of reflections, but shoot them anyway.
- **During a layout, touch only the screen, and lightly. Never push on the phone body.** Don't touch the sprays, the pad, the balls or the boards. If something gets bumped, note it; the tape crosses let me re-align small shifts.

**Each layout, in this order (15 frames):**

| Order | Shot | Lights | Lens polariser | Locking and frames |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **(a) Backlit**, for the cut-out and glow-through | Pad **on**; lamps **off** | X (doesn't matter here) | Press-hold on the pad. **3 frames: −1, 0, +2.** Then set ± back to 0. |
| 2 | **(b) Crossed top light**, for the true colour without shine | Pad **off**; L and R **on** | **X** | Press-hold on the lit sprays. **2 frames: 0, then −1.** |
| 3 | **(c) Parallel top light**, for gloss | Same | Turn the ring to **=** | **Do NOT re-lock.** Shoot −1 first (the slider is already there), then slide to 0 and shoot. **2 frames.** Gloss is measured by comparing (b) with (c), so they must share one lock. |
| 4 | **(d) Raking**, for bumps (normals) | L and R **off**; the **raking lamp only, no film**, moved to spots **1→8** in turn | Leave the ring alone | ± at 0. Re-lock **once** at spot 1, then don't change it. **8 frames**, one per spot. |
| 5 | — | — | Turn back to **X** for the next layout, with ± at 0 | — |

- **Raking has no film on purpose.** A lamp's film turns with the lamp as it moves around the circle. At some spots it would end up crossed with the lens, and the balls' dots would vanish. Unfiltered light keeps the dots visible at every spot, and the software needs those dots. Some shine stays in these frames; if the bump maps come out noisy, I'll ask for a redo.
- If a BP66 is your raking lamp, flip its film up for raking, then back down, and move the lamp back to its L or R mark for the next layout.
- **Each layout takes about 12–15 minutes.** Look through the frames only at the end of each layout.

## 5. Timing and weather

- **Best light:**
  - **Bright overcast** for Parts 1 and 2. It's soft and even, with no hard shadows, and gives the truest colour and shape.
  - **Sun** only for Part 3.
- **Wind:** calm, ideally under about 15 km/h on the Environment Canada hourly forecast. Moving sprays blur, and the phone merges several frames.
- **Wet or frosty foliage:** wait until it's **dry**. Morning frost and dew mean starting after about 10 am. Skip rain days.
- **Hours:** overcast work 10 am–3 pm. For sun work, see Part 3.
  - Sunset is about **6:15 pm** in late October.
  - **The clocks go back on Sunday 1 November.** Sunset is about **5:05 pm** after that **[approximate]**.
- **Frost deadline:**
  - Barrie's average first frost falls in early to mid October. PlantMaps' sources disagree, giving Oct 1–10 or Oct 11–20, so it has likely already happened.
  - For 'Smaragd', a light frost isn't the problem. The risks are **winter colour change** (some cedars turn bronze in the cold) and **snow**.
  - 'Smaragd' is usually said to hold its green better than most cedars, but **[unsure]** I don't know when it starts to bronze in Barrie.
  - Aim to finish by about **7 November**.
- **Plan:**
  1. Check your phone model, then order. Most items arrive Oct 21–23, and the lamps on Oct 28.
  2. Set up the station and do the dry run as soon as the lamps arrive, ideally the day before the shoot.
  3. Parts 1 and 2 on the first good overcast day. The pad station must be within about 30 minutes of the hedge.
  4. Part 3 on the next calm, sunny day.

| Rig set-up and polariser check (day before) | about 30 min |
| Dry run (same session) | about 30 min |
| Part 1, hedge (overcast) | about 95 min (measuring 25–30, whole hedge 35, close-ups 10, base 10, cutting 10) |
